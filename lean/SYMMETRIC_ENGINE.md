# The symmetric engine: design note

Written 2026-11-13 against master `ffaac8a`, for roadmap milestone M3 part A (the engine design note) and milestone M6 (ledger L01, L20, L29). It confirms the early verdict in `symmetric-engine/lineage-verdict.md` of the project files and reverses nothing in it.

Everything marked *measured* was run and is reproducible from this repository. Everything marked *argued* is an argument on paper. Nothing here is proved in Lean.

## 1. Decision

M6 builds one new machine, with these five properties.

1. **It evaluates a normal form, not the syntax tree.** Before the first step, program and query are rewritten so that `;` is a flat list, `&` is a multiset, `|` is a set, and converse occurs only on calls. Two inputs that differ in the bracketing of `;`, in the order or bracketing of `&` or `|`, give the same first state.
2. **It picks one orientation of the whole input.** The normal form of the input and the normal form of its mirror image are both built and the smaller, in a fixed structural order, is run; answers are swapped back if the mirror image was chosen. A query and its mirror image are then the same run.
3. **A goal and its converse share one table.**
4. **A chain goal is a table on an interval of a flat chain.** Every split of the interval is a strategy of that table, and so is pushing the answers of a complete side into the other side, from either end. The strategies of a table are balanced by the ticks they have spent (since the fourth revision of section 3: the splits of a table are balanced by their levels). No strategy is chosen by syntax.
5. **Nothing runs beside the tables.** There is no unfolding race, no mirror race and no hedge of whole machines. *Amended on 2026-11-15 (fourth revision of that day, section 3): the unfolding race is a strategy of every table. The interface check did not cover it.*

Points 1 to 3 give the exact properties (mirror image step for step at equal ticks, `SeqAssoc`, `AndComm`, `AndAssoc`). Points 4 and 5 give the cost. The two are separate, and section 3 explains why that separation is the main finding.

**Revision, 2026-11-14: points 2 and 3 as built.** Point 2 as written leaves two things open, and point 3 does not close them.

*The engine cannot make the choice.* An engine is handed the root node and reads bodies through the kernel. It cannot build the mirrored program, so it cannot compare the input with its mirror image. The first form of the machine, run on `add ; @2` and on its mirror image, shows different traces even at a kernel that charges nothing, because it visits members in their stored order. So the choice is made where the input is loaded (`orient` and `CostedRuntime.oriented`, `Trusted/Cost.lean`), and a rung says whether it is run that way (`CostedRung.oriented`).

*An input can be its own mirror image.* Then there is nothing to choose between, the run is one run, and the criterion asks that this run shows, at every step, a set of answers that is its own converse. Take `u = a -> b | b -> a` and `w = b -> c | c -> b`, and the query `u ; w ; w ; u`. Program and query are their own mirror images. The splits `u | w ; w ; u` and `u ; w ; w | u` are mirror images of each other, and they are balanced by the ticks they spend. A kernel may charge an operation and its mirror image differently (nothing in `Kernel.Refines` says otherwise, and the 2026-11-13 revision of section 6 withdrew the request that it should not), so the two splits get different levels and an answer can arrive requests before its converse. Sharing a table between a goal and its converse does not help: the converse of `u ; w ; w` is `w° ; w° ; u°`, a different node from `w ; w ; u`, and only the program being its own mirror image makes them the same relation.

What is built:

1. *Four inputs, not two.* An input stands with its mirror image (the mirrored query in the mirrored program), with its converse (the converse query in the same program), and with the converse of its mirror image. The loader compares the program with the mirrored program body by body in normal form and runs the lesser; the candidates for the root are the query and its converse, and in a program that is its own mirror image the mirrored query and its converse too. The least candidate is run, and what the run shows is swapped when that candidate relates the pairs the other way round. The choice reads normal forms only, and it is the same from each of the four. This gives the criterion on the mirrored input and on the converse query at once, with equal ticks on the mirrored input, for any kernel.
2. *When the least candidate stands both ways round*, the relation is its own converse, and the run is of the union of that node with its converse. That node is its own converse.
3. *The table of a goal that is its own converse holds the converse of everything it holds.* Whenever an answer goes in, its converse goes in with it (`addAnswer`, `Rungs/Symmetric/Machine.lean`). So a run whose root is its own converse shows, at every step, a set of answers that is its own converse, whatever the kernel charges and whatever order the machine visits things in.

Point 3 of the decision (a goal and its converse share one table) is then no part of any exact property. It remains a saving and is not built. Whether it is one is not known: the kernel gives equality of nodes and no order, so every opening of a goal would also look for the converse goal, which doubles the comparisons of every lookup, against at most a factor of two on runs that open both a goal and its converse. It is on the billboard as SIM12 and is not owed by any criterion of M6.

Two ways not taken. Asking every kernel to charge an operation and its mirror image the same would make the machine symmetric with no choice of orientation, but only if the machine never reads a stored order: every request would have to read what the tables held when it started, which costs requests in every recursion, and every later rung would owe the same proof. Balancing by the number of operations instead of their ticks is symmetric at any kernel and gives up the cost bound, since one operation can cost any amount.

F1 below stands as corrected here: the orientation makes the mirror image the same run, except where the input is its own mirror image, and there the exact property comes from item 3.

Not chosen: full direction-free nets, the old engine's two-ended alternation as an evaluation order, and any hedge. Section 4 compares them.

## 2. What the exit criteria force

Three facts follow from M6's exit criteria alone, before any design.

**F1. Exact symmetry is a property of the representation, not of the search policy.** *Argued.* No total order on spans is preserved by swapping (for `l ≠ r`, the spans `l -> r` and `r -> l` would each have to be below the other). So a machine that visits tables, answers or branches in any stored order cannot have its mirror-image run be literally its run reflected, and a proof of equal ticks for it has to show every traversal is insensitive to order. A machine that is given the same state for both writings needs no such proof. That is why the decision normalises and orients the input instead of asking the policy to be symmetric.

**F2. With F1 in place, cost-based choices are free.** `r005/design-notes.md` finding 2 says a hedge must preempt by actual cost, and that this breaks step-for-step `DualTrace` unless a run and its mirror image cost exactly the same. Under points 1 and 2 they cost exactly the same for every policy, because they are the same run. So the M6 engine and every rung above it may schedule by ticks.

**F3. The engine must be within a polynomial of the yardstick on the cheapest writing of the input.** *Argued.* The engine's run does not depend on the writing. `Simulates` must hold against the yardstick (R005) on every writing. So the engine's cost is bounded by a polynomial of the least cost R005 has over all bracketings, both directions and all orders of `&`. There are exponentially many writings, so this cannot be had by racing them. It is the theorem M6 has to prove and its main risk (section 7).

A consequence of F3: an engine that picks one bracketing by a rule, however symmetric the rule, fails, because R005 run on a better bracketing beats it. The choice has to be made by cost, per goal, while running.

## 3. The machine

### Normal form

A node is one of: a span; a call, with an orientation bit; a chain (a list of at least two nodes, none of them a chain); a meet (a multiset of at least two nodes, none of them a meet); a union (a set of nodes, none of them a union; the empty union is `zero`). Converse is pushed inward: it reverses a chain, maps over a meet or a union, swaps a span, and flips the bit of a call. A call with its bit set reads the converse of the definition's body.

Nodes are interned, so equal nodes are one identifier. Every sub-interval of a chain is a node too, so equal stretches of two different bodies share their tables. Sets and multisets are ordered by a fixed structural order when they are interned; the order is used for nothing else.

Adjacent spans are **not** fused during normalisation. Fusing them would make the first state depend on composition and simplification being associative up to equivalence in every theory, which is not known before M5. The interval tables compose them anyway.

### Goals and tables

A goal is `⟨lb, node, rb⟩` with optional boundaries, as in R001. Its key is the node's identifier and the canonical representatives of the two boundaries (M5). The goal `⟨rbᵒ, nodeᵒ, lbᵒ⟩` has the same table, read swapped. A table holds answers, a completion flag, and what each of its strategies has spent.

### Strategies

| Node | Strategies of `⟨lb, node, rb⟩` |
|---|---|
| span | Compose with the boundaries. |
| call | The body's goal with the same boundaries. |
| union | The members' goals with the same boundaries. All run; a union is not a choice. |
| chain `[i, j)` | For each split `k`: join the answers of `⟨lb, [i,k), none⟩` and `⟨none, [k,j), rb⟩`. For each split `k` and each end: push the interface of each answer of that side into the other side as a boundary, as the answers arrive, and waiting only for the side's maximal answers once it is complete (thirteenth revision) (R001's push, and the rung 6 thread's push without join, `Directed.Gen.seqCandidates`). |
| meet | For each member: its goal with both boundaries, then push each answer's two interfaces into the remaining members as one meet goal. Join of two halves for every split of the multiset into two is **not** offered (there are exponentially many); see open point O3. |

### Balance

Work is done one kernel operation at a time. A goal that is asked to work passes the request to the strategy of its table that has spent least; where several are level, each of them is advanced. A strategy that has a pair of answers not yet combined combines one pair. A strategy with nothing to combine passes the request to the sub-goal it has fed less. A strategy charges itself with everything spent below it on its behalf. A request to the root goal is one step of the machine.

A table is complete when one of its strategies has combined everything from complete sub-tables (for a union, when all members are complete), and by R001's exact check on the whole state for goals that depend on themselves. Nothing more is spent on a complete table, and its other strategies are dropped.

**Revision, 2026-11-13: requests alternate between two rules.** The rule above (the *share* rule) halves a goal's share at every level of nesting, so an answer under `k` nested choices that each have a member which never finishes arrives after about `2^k` requests (section 5.4). So every second request is instead one *flat round*: each opened goal that is not complete does one unit of its own work. The flat round alone is slow when many goals are open, which is where the share rule is good; alternating costs at most twice the better of the two. A strategy's spent ticks count work done under either rule.

**Revision, 2026-11-13 (third): the interface check, whole rounds, and no claim about alternating.** From the attack on O1 (`SYMMETRIC_ENGINE_SIMULATION.md`, which has the measurements). Three changes.

1. *A new strategy, the interface check.* For each split of a chain goal and each side: the first (or last) span of every answer of that side is one of finitely many spans known from the program text, found by following the edge of the node inwards, composing adjacent spans, through unions and through each call once. The other side is opened under the interface of each of those spans, as ordinary goals with a boundary. If all of them complete with no answer, the chain goal is complete and empty. Without it the engine never halts on `p ; q` when every answer of `p` ends in a constructor that no answer of `q` begins with and both have infinitely many answers; R005 halts there in its second round, by unfolding, so the engine as first designed neither simulated R005 nor halted wherever R001 halts. The check reads no bracketing.
2. *The flat round is a whole round:* each strategy of each open goal does everything its inputs allow. With one unit per goal, a table can gain answers faster than its reader reads them, and the fair OR family with three members that never finish takes 12, 39, 304 requests for `k = 2, 4, 6`; with whole rounds every fair OR family measured takes `2k + 7`. A whole round is a round of R005's machine on every split at once that repeats no pair.
3. *The sentence "alternating costs at most twice the better of the two" is withdrawn.* It is false by requests (a flat round costs as many ticks as there are goals) and false when the rules are balanced by ticks, because both rules write the same tables: counting down halts at 10398 ticks for `k = 8` under whole rounds alone and at 979757 when they are balanced with the share rule. Which rule serves a request is therefore open again (O6 below). The share rule alone is exponential to halt on counting down, where R005 is polynomial; whole rounds alone are exponential on the middle-restricted family with pushes, where the share rule is polynomial.

What this forces to be redone: nothing built. Section 5.4 measured a model of the rules and is superseded by tables 4 to 6b of the simulation note. The freeze list of section 6 is unchanged: the interface check uses goals, boundaries and `join` as they are, and its conclusion (a chain is empty because every interface of one side fails on the other) is a completion, not an answer, so the derivation kernel needs no new rule.

**Revision, 2026-11-13 (fourth): the split balance replaces the share rule and the flat round, and the interface check is narrowed.** From the second attack on O1 (`SYMMETRIC_ENGINE_SIMULATION.md`, section 9, which has the measurements).

*Why.* R005 was ported out of Lean (`scripts/r005_interpreter.py`, which reproduces the 57 numbers Lean guards for the inputs of the simulation note) and run on the ladder family. R005 is polynomial on it (about the third power of `n`, to `n = 34`). The engine of the third revision is exponential on it under whole rounds, under the share rule, under unit rounds and under both rules that had been left untried. The cause is a split that the cheap writing does not have: `G ; F | r` has a left side that completes, its answers are pushed into `r`, and each pushed goal has the same split again, which is a forward search with `2^d` goals at depth `d`. Whole rounds serve all of it; the share rule halves the useful line at every level. So reading every split, which is what gives `SeqAssoc`, does cost more than a polynomial under those rules. It is not yet a conflict between `SeqAssoc` and `Simulates`, because the rule below repairs it and reads no bracketing.

*The request rule.* Everything said above under "Balance" about which strategy gets a request, and both earlier revisions of it, are replaced by this.

1. The strategies of one split of a chain goal (its join and its two pushes) are a *group*. The interface checks of a chain goal are one more group. A goal that is not a chain has one group.
2. Every group has a *level*, a number kept in the goal's table. The level of a goal is the lowest level among its groups.
3. A request to the root is a round. In its round a goal lets only its lowest groups work; if none of them can do anything, the next lowest, and so on. A group works by doing its own work and giving a round to every goal it reads. *(Since the eleventh revision of 2026-11-15 a union gives a round to one member, the one its rounds have cost the least.)* A goal has at most one round per request, whoever asks.
4. The level of a group rises by the ticks of its own work and by how much the levels of the goals it reads rose in the round. A goal met again below itself adds nothing.
5. Where a goal has one group, the group does everything its inputs allow. Where it has several, each strategy of a working group does one unit.

A split is charged for the *lowest* group of each goal it reads, not for everything spent there. So the hedging done below a split is not charged to it, the allowance of its competitors does not grow by a factor per level of nesting, and a search that only one split starts is held to the level of the cheapest split. A request is not divided among the goals a group reads, so nothing is starved by a sibling that never finishes. *(Since the eleventh revision of 2026-11-15 a union divides the ticks evenly among its members. Nothing else is divided.)*

*Measured* on the prototype (table 8 of the simulation note): polynomial on the ladder (to `n = 64`), on counting down, on the middle-restricted family with pushes (about the sixth power to `k = 14`, where the share rule had the fourth and R005 triples per step), and on fair OR with one and with three members that never finish, where the answer arrives in `2k + 4` requests. It is the first rule that has both the middle-restricted family and fair OR with three such members. Nothing is proved.

*The interface check is narrowed.* It is offered only for a side that contains a call, and its goals are opened when it first runs, at one tick each. Offered for every side it is exponential on the middle-restricted family under every round rule (9346012 ticks and 253966 goals at `k = 7`), which the third revision did not measure far enough to see; the prototype also opened its goals at no charge. A side with no call has finitely many answers, its table completes, and the push from it opens the other side under exact interfaces, so the check adds nothing there. With the checks as a group of their own, a check is held to the level of the cheapest split. `p ; q` halts at 128 ticks and its variant with identity spans at 342.

What this forces to be redone: nothing built. Levels are numbers in tables and are read with `metered`; the freeze list of section 6 is unchanged. The L36 row's sentence about two request rules giving one bound each is superseded: one rule, measured against the flat bound only (section 9.7 of the simulation note). The share bound (the product of live alternatives along the nesting) is no longer claimed by any rule of the design; whether the split balance has it was not measured.

The balance takes one weight per strategy and uses equal weights. Other weights are roadmap item L32 and need no new machine.

**Revision, 2026-11-14 (second): the interface check as built, and a fair order of units.** The check is in `Rungs/Symmetric/Machine.lean` (`runs`, `readEdge`, the strategy `check`), tested in `Tests/SymmetricRuns.lean`, and covered by the invariant and the mirror criterion (`Goals.lean`). Six points differ from, or add to, the fourth revision and the prototype.

1. *The reading has no bound to run under, and needs no new kernel operation.* A node is an identifier and the kernel does not tell an engine how deep the program is, so a traversal of the text cannot be written as one total computation. A reading is given a depth and says when the depth was not enough. The state holds the depth readings are tried at, starting at 1, and doubles it whenever a reading fails. A depth larger than needed costs nothing, and a failed reading is at a depth below the depth of the program, so the depth held is always less than twice that. A check whose reading failed tries again in its next unit of work. The alternative was a trusted operation giving a bound on the nesting of the program. It would have made the reading immediate, and it would have added to the trusted interface for one engine.
2. *What is read is kept* per node and end, so a side is read once however many goals stand on its node, and once the depth of the program has been reached every side is read when the groups of its goal are made, as in the prototype. Until then a chain goal carries checks that are still reading.
3. *The reading is charged.* The prototype read an edge at no charge. Here it costs what the kernel charges for the views, the leaves and the compositions it does, and that goes to the level of the group of checks.
4. *A group of checks none of which can do anything more is removed from its table.* A check is dead when it is not offered or when one of its goals has an answer. The prototype keeps such a group, with its level frozen; the level of the goal is the lowest level of its groups, so there the goal's level stops rising, and whoever reads the goal is no longer charged for it. The fourth revision did not choose between the two. Measured on the prototype with the option `drop_dead`: no change on the middle-restricted family, on fair OR or on `p ; q`; a fifth more ticks on counting down, with the same requests; on the ladder 1196396 ticks against 632782 at `n = 32`, 593720 against 331597 at `n = 24` and 182590 against 158004 at `n = 16`, so between the second and the third power either way. Removal is kept: with the group frozen, a goal whose checks are dead is free to everything that reads it, which is what the ladder gains from, and it is the balance switched off for those goals, not a rule.
5. *A side with no product at all completes its chain.* If the reading finds that no product of the side exists (adjacent spans at its edge do not compose), the check has no goal to open and is complete at once. The prototype made no check there. A meet is not read: a check on a side that is a meet, or whose edge is one, is not offered.

6. *The order of the units is fair, and the old order lost pairs.* This is not about the check; it was found on starting the proof that the engine is fair. Where a goal has several groups a strategy does one unit at a time, and the prototype's join took its units with the first side first. Two things are wrong with that. A pair is skipped for good when the first side gains an answer while the pairs of an answer of the second side are half done, because the count of pairs done is then read as a count for the first side; and the second side waits for ever while the first gains answers faster than they are joined. A push read its first goal first, which starves the others the same way. Now a join finishes the pairs of one answer before it begins another and begins on the side it has counted fewer answers of, and a push (and a call or a union, where they work by units) reads next the table it has read least. `Tests/SymmetricRuns.lean` has the cases, on the cursor itself. Measured on the prototype with the option `fair_units`: no change on counting down, fair OR and `p ; q`, and within a tenth on the middle-restricted family and on the ladder, in both directions.

Measured on the engine at the domain of sorts with only the node operations charged: `p ; q` halts at request 10, and its variant with identity spans at request 4 (the prototype: 6 and 6). Counting down answers at request `2k + 10` and halts after `7k + 12`; without the check the engine had `2k + 7` and `5k + 9`, and the prototype with the check has `2k + 7` and `6k + 8`. The three requests on the first answer are the learning of the depth. The slope of the halt differs from the prototype's because the levels are in different ticks. Fair OR with one and with three endless members answers no later than counting down alone. The ladder and the middle-restricted family have not been run on the engine itself; the prototype's numbers for them are for readings at no charge.

What this forces to be redone: nothing. O7 is unchanged: that the check covers the unfolding race is still not proved.

**Revision, 2026-11-14 (third): the engine is fair and complete.** Proved in `Rungs/Symmetric` (`Turns.lean`, `ReadLive.lean`, `Lines.lean`, `Served.lean`, `Fair.lean`, `Correct.lean`), for every lawful domain and with no use of the levels, so it does not depend on the balance or on the weights:

1. *The record of a request* (`Req`, `req_request`). The groups of a table change in its own round only. In its round every strategy moves on, one group works with the tables it reads settled (since the eleventh revision of 2026-11-15, for a union: fed, `Fed`), and only a group of dead checks is removed. A table marked complete has a strategy with nothing left to do that reads complete tables only.
2. *A table that has a round in infinitely many requests has a group that works in infinitely many* (`Requests.line`): the number of its groups stops falling, and then one place works again and again.
3. *Such a group reaches everything* (`IsRun.served_live`). A span has run. A call or a union reads every answer: what it has not read below a position falls whenever the group works. A join combines every pair: the rank of its cursor falls. Of a group of checks one is never dead; the depth doubles while it has not read its side, and from the depth `readNeed` a reading does not give up (`readEdge_some`), so it reads it, is offered, and none of its goals ever has an answer. The tables these strategies read need no round in infinitely many requests, so each is complete at some time or has rounds for ever.
4. *A complete table is served by the strategy that completed it* (`IsRun.served_done`).
5. So a run of requests is saturated (`Saturation.Saturated`, `IsRun.saturated`), with the tables that are complete at some time or have rounds for ever as the goals it opens. The root is one of them, and the machine has emitted every answer of the root table at the latest when it reports that it is done. Hence `Symmetric.complete`, `Symmetric.honestExhaustion` and `Symmetric.honestProgress`.

A table that is opened and then has only finitely many rounds without becoming complete is not in that set, and nothing is claimed of it: no strategy that serves a table reads one.

No answer of a table covers a later one (`nocov_stateAt`), so `Symmetric.finalMaximal` holds too.

Still owed for `Keeps`: `TerminatesWhere` (which needs O7) and `EmptySide`.

**Revision, 2026-11-15: work that is done is counted, and every group works for ever.** Found on starting `EmptySide`, which needs more of the balance than completeness did: completeness needs one group of a table to work for ever, and halting needs the group that would complete the table to work.

1. *The fault.* A level rose by what the kernel charged for the work. A kernel may charge nothing (`Kernel.Refines` says nothing of the ticks, and the kernel the engine is run at in `Tests/SymmetricRuns.lean` charges nothing for an answer). A group that only combines answers then worked without its level rising, stayed lowest, and the rule "only the lowest groups work, the next lowest if none of them can do anything" kept every other group of its table from working for as long as it had pairs to combine, which can be for ever. On counting down, groups did work in requests in which their level did not rise (`workCounted`, which failed).
2. *The repair.* The engine charges itself one tick for every unit of work that did something (`workStrats`), and the tick is counted in the level. So a group did something exactly when its level rose, at every kernel (`workGroup_did`). Nothing else changes; a group that could do nothing still costs nothing.
3. *What is proved from it.* The record of a round has the balance (`Balanced`): each group worked, or it stands as it stood while a group that stood lower rose. Hence every group of a table that has rounds for ever works for ever (`Requests.lines`): a group that never works again keeps its level, the sum over the groups of what each holds below that level grows in every round, and it is bounded. This uses the levels and no weights other than: work that did something costs something.
4. *Measured again* on the engine at the domain of sorts: counting down answers at request `2k + 9` and halts after about `7k + 10` (25, 38, 66, 93 for `k = 2, 4, 8, 12`; before `2k + 10` and `7k + 12`); `p ; q` halts at request 11 (before 10) and its variant with identity spans at request 9 (before 4, the one case that is clearly later); fair OR answers no later than counting down alone, as before. The prototype's tables were measured in kernel ticks that are never zero, so they stand as measurements of the rule; its numbers are not the engine's, as before.

What this forces to be redone: nothing proved. The guards on request counts in `Tests/SymmetricRuns.lean` are the new numbers.

Planned for `EmptySide`: a table that completes in the run on `a` completes in every run that keeps giving it rounds, in the manner of `Rungs/Tabled/Host.lean`. That needs the pushes in the record of a round and the order in which tables complete. Both are now proved:

- *Pushes in the record* (`SStep.push`, `step_work`). A push that works while its side is complete: takes the maximal answers of the side if it had not; else opens the goal of the next of them; else, with a goal open for each, is nearer to every answer those goals have. `SStep` is given which tables are complete for this. After a group works, the goals a push has opened need no round, once it has taken the answers of its side (`SFed`).
- *The order of completion* (`Ranked`, `Founded`, `IsRun.founded`). A table is marked complete in two places: in its own round, when a strategy has nothing left to do and reads complete tables only, and at the end of a request, with the tables that complete together. From the record of a request alone a set of tables could look complete because of itself, for instance two tables each pushing the answers of the other. So the record carries this: the complete tables of a state stand in an order, each with a strategy with nothing left to do that reads only complete tables standing no higher, and a table whose answers are pushed stands strictly lower. It holds because `quiet` asks of a push that its side is already marked complete. A table that newly completes stands above all that were complete (`Founded.step`).

**Revision, 2026-11-15 (second): what one run completes, every run completes.** Proved (`Rungs/Symmetric/Transfer.lean`, `IsRun.transfer`), for every lawful domain: a goal whose table is complete at some time in a run of requests has a complete table in every run of requests of the same program that does not neglect the goal (its table is complete at some time or has a round in infinitely many requests) and in which no answer of a table covers a later one, which holds of the states of the engine (`nocov_stateAt`). The two runs need not start from the same root, open the same goals, or work them in the same order. What the proof needed, each proved:

1. *What an edge reads is a fact of the program* (`EdgeSame.lean`, `Reads.unique`). Two readings that do not give up return the same, at any depths and any kernels that return what the domain returns. Every check records what was read (`Prog`). So a check opens the same goals in both runs, and a check that is not offered in one run is not offered in the other.
2. *A run keeps a strategy for every way to answer a goal* (`Full`, in `Good`): its span, its call or its union, and for every split the join and both pushes, and for a split of a chain both checks. A group is removed only when all its checks are dead, and a dead check can never show the goal empty (`CheckGone`); the transfer shows that a check that completed the goal in one run is never dead in the other.
3. *A join that has combined every pair has nothing left to combine, and stays so.* The record of a round did not say this: `SStep.join` said only that the rank of the cursor falls while a given pair is not combined, which leaves a cursor with every pair combined and its counts behind. It now says that the counts never fall, and that a join that works has nothing left to combine or combined a pair it had not (`Cursor.next_step`).

**Revision, 2026-11-15 (eleventh): the members of a union share the ticks.** Fairness is one law, fair OR, stated in ticks (`Fair.share`, `Trusted/Properties.lean`): an answer costs at most a constant times what it costs when its alternative is the whole query, times the number of members of each union at which it was chosen. 2. **Revision, 2026-11-16 (twelfth): a join shares its ticks by where its answers came from.** The engine of the eleventh revision does not meet fair OR on a chain of two unions. On `(a1 | a2) ; (b1 | b2)` with the alternative `a1 ; b1`, where `a2` and `b2` never finish, the cost over the cost of the alternative alone grew with the input: 11, 14, 18, 27, 36 at `k` = 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, and the law allows a constant times 4. The join of the two union tables tried every pair of their answers in the order of arrival, so the one pair wanted waited for the pairs among the answers of the members that never finish, and there are as many of those as the square of the time.

1. An answer has a *path*: for each union on the way to it, the member chosen and the number of members, the outermost first (`Table.paths`, `stamp`). A union puts its choice before the path of the answer it copies. A call keeps the path. A join puts the paths of its two answers one after the other. An answer that is derived again keeps its first path.
2. A join files the answers of its two tables by their paths as it meets them (`Cursor.see`), so the path it knows for an answer never changes. The *steps* of a pair are the choices of its first answer, then those of its second, then an end mark (`stepsOf`). A *class* is a beginning of the steps of a pair: the pairs whose answers came from those choices.
3. The join keeps what each class was fed, the ticks its pairs cost, and combines next the pair of the least key (`Cursor.next`, `Cursor.keyOf`). The key compares the classes of two pairs from the widest down. At the first depth where they part, the class with the earlier *place* wins: what it was fed and one tick more, once for each member of its union and once more, and then its member (`Cursor.place`). Among the members of one union that is the order by what each was fed. Inside one class the pair of the earliest answers comes first. A class has no work but that of its pairs, so the shares multiply to the number of members at each choice on the way to a pair, which is the factor of the law.
4. The bookkeeping is not charged: the kernel does no work in it. An implementation keeps the classes in a trie and finds the next pair in time linear in the depth of the paths.

*Every pair is still reached* (`Cursor.rank_fall`, `Cursor.lean`), with no bound on how many members a union has. The measure of a pair is the number of pairs of earlier answers not combined, and then, for each class of the pair from the narrowest to the widest, how far the siblings that could come before it are from places after its own. Only finitely many places come before a place, which is why the place counts the members and not only the ticks. A unit that combines another pair either stays in the classes of the pair to the end, and is then an earlier pair, or leaves them at some depth for a sibling that was placed no later and is placed later afterwards, while nothing narrower is touched. The proofs of fairness and completeness stand with that measure in place of the old rank (`SStep.join`, `Line.join_done`, `IsRun.live_join`).

*Not done here.* A push still gives a round to every goal it opened, one for each answer pushed, so many pushed answers from a member not chosen still delay the answer wanted by their number; pushes are next. The law is not proved for the engine (L36). An answer derived twice is filed under its first path only; no input has been found on which that breaks the law, and none is proved not to.

*The build runs nothing.* The checks on concrete inputs under `lean/Tests` are removed, this engine's among them. The figures in this note are measurements taken once on the commit they name.

**Revision, 2026-11-16 (thirteenth): a push opens goals as answers arrive, and drops covered answers once its side is complete.** Three findings and what is built.

*A push that waits for its whole side breaks fair OR.* On the prototype, `(@w | junk) ; q` with a member `junk` that never finishes: the side `@w | junk` never completes, so the answer of `@w` was never pushed, and the cost over the cost of `@w ; q` alone grew from 2.7 to 56 at `k` = 2 to 12 where the law allows a constant times 2 (`fairness/measurements.md` in the project files).

*Opening a goal for every answer on arrival, and waiting for all of them, breaks halting.* Not run; by reasoning on the engine as defined. Take `p = (q ; p) | gen` and `q = spec1 | spec2 | general`, where `q` answers `(a, g(f(a)))`, `(g(f(Z)), g(f(f(Z))))` and `(X, g(Y))`, and `gen` has endlessly many answers, none under a left boundary `g(..)`. Run `p` under the left boundary `a`. The engine that waits pushes only the maximal answer `(a, g(Y))`, the goal it opens comes back to itself, and the run halts. An engine that pushes on arrival and waits for every goal it opened, when the specific answers arrive first, opens `p` under `g(f(a))`, then under `g(f(f(a)))`, and so on for ever. Halting would then depend on the order of arrival, and the certificate that carries completion from one run to another (`Cert`, `Why.push`) speaks of the maximal answers of a complete side only. So a push must stop waiting for the goal of an answer that another covers.

*Testing for covered answers on every arrival costs too much.* Measured on the Python prototype only (`fairness/T6.py`, `exp50.py`, `exp51.py` in the project files). The first engine built for this revision tested each new answer of the side against the earlier ones as it arrived. On a side that never completes the tests grow with the square of the answers seen. Work for `need` beside an endless sibling, alone and whole, at `k` = 2, 4, 6: the prototype's arrival push with no cover tests and feeding shared by classes 1192/1407, 4172/4294, 16503/18974; tests on arrival 2527/3206, 20128/44095, 190645/229370; tests once at completion 1374/1723, 8523/12337, 50780/62701; tests once at completion with feeding shared by classes, the same as with no tests. Counting down to a halt at `k` = 4, 8, 16: no tests and feeding by classes 8713, 45479, 218721; tests on arrival 10336, 71313, 574754; tests at completion 9423, 41947, 269942. The tests are needed only to stop waiting, and a push can only stop once its side is complete, so they are made then.

*As built* (`Rungs/Symmetric/Machine.lean`, `Strat.push`, `superseded`, `supersede`, `seeSide`, `pushFile`, `Cursor.kill`). A push keeps the answers of its side it has seen, in the order of the table, each with the table opened for it once there is one and how many of that table's answers are filed; the positions of the answers *superseded*, those a later answer covers, which is nothing until the side is complete; the units filed, one that opens the goal of each answer seen and one that combines each answer filed of an opened table; and a cursor over the units of the same kind as the join's. At each unit of work it sees the new answers of its side, files the new answers of the opened tables, and takes the next unit in the cursor's order: it opens a goal or combines one answer. The first time it works with its side complete it makes the cover tests over the whole side, once, and from then on drops the units of the superseded answers and no longer feeds, reads or waits for their goals. The push has nothing left to do when its side is complete, the tests are made, every answer is seen, no unit is left, and every answer not superseded has its table read to the end. The answers not superseded are then the side's maximal answers, each once, so what the push waits for at the end is what the old push waited for.

*What it costs.* The cover tests are the ones the old push made, at the same moment, and are charged. Goals opened for answers that turn out to be covered are worked until the side completes; on a side that never completes they are worked for ever, each with its share. The prototype figures above put this at about a quarter more than the prototype's push with no tests on halting runs, and about three times on the endless sibling at `k` = 6 until feeding is shared by classes.

*Proved, with the full build passing and nothing run.* The record of a step (`step_work_push`, `PAdv`, `PStep`, `Turns.lean`): every unit not yet taken is taken, or is of an answer superseded, or is nearer its turn, strictly so when the push works; the superseded positions, once computed, are kept; and a push that works with its side complete has computed them. A push whose group works for ever sees every answer of a complete side and has made its tests (`Line.push_seen`), then opens the goal of each not superseded (`Line.push_opened`), then files and combines every answer of those goals once they gain no more (`Line.push_done`, `Settle.lean`). A push with nothing left to do serves its goal (`IsRun.served_push`): every answer of the side is covered by one not superseded (`DeadOk.live`), in a lawful domain, which `IsRun.fair` now asks for. What one run completes every run completes (`IsRun.transfer`), since the answers not superseded of a complete side are maximal (`Cert.waits_quiet`) and every maximal answer has one of them with the same span (`IsRun.fcert`). So the statements of the seventh to ninth revisions stand for this engine: it is fair and complete, it halts wherever R005 halts, and it keeps every property on one orientation.

*Not done here.* The goals a push has opened are each given a round when the push is fed; sharing that feeding by where the pushed answers came from is next, and the prototype says it brings the cost on the endless sibling back to that of a push with no tests. The law is not proved for the engine (L36).

**Revision, 2026-11-16 (fourteenth): the goals a push opened share their rounds by where the pushed answers came from.** The push of the thirteenth revision gave a round to every goal it had opened each time its group was fed. A member of the side that never finishes sends answers for ever, each opens a goal, and the one goal wanted then waits for a round of every one of them: the same fault the twelfth revision removed from the join, one level down.

*As built* (`Rungs/Symmetric/Machine.lean`, the last field of `Strat.push`, `syncFeed`, `fileToks`, `feedToks`, `feedPush`, `feedPushes`, `feedGroup`, `reFeed`). A push has a second cursor, its *feed cursor*, of the same kind as the join's. A unit of it stands for one round offered to the table opened for one answer of the side, and its class is the path of that answer, so the units are taken in the order the pairs of a join are (third point of the twelfth revision). When a group is fed, the side of each push has its round as before. Then, for each push: the cursor is given the path of every answer seen; the units of the superseded answers are dropped; a unit is filed for each opened table that is not complete, is not of a superseded answer and has no unit; and units are taken in the cursor's order, each giving its table a round and being marked with what the round cost, until a round has cost something or no unit is left. A table whose unit was taken gets a new one the next time the group is fed. The work of a push never touches its feed cursor, and the feeding touches nothing else of a push (`work_feedOf`, `feedGroup_core`).

*Every opened goal still has rounds for ever*, with no bound on how many answers a side has. This is the measure of the twelfth revision used again: while the table of an answer that is never superseded waits, the answer has a unit, the unit is not taken, and each time the tables of the push share rounds the unit is strictly nearer its turn (`Cursor.rank_fall_todo`, `Cursor.rank_mark_zero`, `turn_feedToks`); the measure cannot fall for ever (`Line.push_fed`, `Settle.lean`). The record of a round says so for each push of a group that worked (`FedP`, the third part of `Tried`, `Turns.lean`), over one working or several in a round (`FedP.trans`), and a group that did not work in a round keeps its strategies as they were (`Balanced`). A feed cursor fits what it is used for throughout (`Cursor.Fits`, in `Prog`; `feedGroup_fits`, `FeedCursor.lean`).

*What it costs.* The bookkeeping of the cursor is not charged, as for the join. Measured on the Python prototype only (`fairness/T6.py` with the feeding as a queue of units, `exp52.py`, in the project files): work for `need` beside an endless sibling, alone and whole, at `k` = 2, 4, 6 is 1192/1407, 4172/4294, 16503/18974, the figures of the push with no cover tests in the thirteenth revision; counting down to a halt at `k` = 4, 8, 16 is 8713, 44882, 215654. The Lean engine has not been run.

*Proved, with the full build passing and nothing run.* Every statement of the thirteenth revision stands for this engine: it is fair and complete, it halts wherever R005 halts, and it keeps every property on one orientation. The one proof that changed in substance is the transfer of completion: that the table opened for a maximal answer is asked for ever was read off the feeding of every opened goal, and is now `Line.push_fed`.

*Not done here.* The weight of a push still counts every goal it opened (`Strat.feeds`), and the law is not proved for the engine (L36).

**Revision, 2026-11-16 (fifteenth): fair OR in ticks is no law.** The eleventh to fourteenth revisions changed how work is shared so as to meet `Fair.share`. That law is removed from the specification (Anthony, 2026-11-16): it was a cheap way to force some optimizations early and stopped being cheap, and as it was worded no machine that stops paying for dead branches can meet it (billboard FR4). Completeness is the law, and the ladder carries cost. The sharing rules of those revisions stay as the engine's schedule; nothing is owed of them beyond what completeness needs (every member that is never settled is fed in infinitely many requests). `Fair`, `Alternative` and `shareLinear_false` are deleted from the Lean development.

**Revision, 2026-11-16 (sixteenth): the engine is rung 1, and is held to the three laws only.** The specification now asks of a rung that it is sound, complete pair by pair, and honest when it reports that it is done (`Runtime.Laws`), and nothing else. The engine with the raced unfolding, no rewrite before it and run on one orientation of its input is rung 1 and the base of the ladder (`Rungs/Symmetric/Rung.lean`, `Symmetric.rung1_lawful`, `rung1_ladder`). Its cost does not depend on the direction a query is written in (`rung1_directionFree`): on an input, on its converse and on its mirror image it covers the same pairs having paid the same, and at most `8 * n + 12` ticks more for choosing the input, `n` the size of the input. This uses that the table of a goal that is its own converse holds the converse of everything it holds (`engine_selfConverse`). What the earlier revisions call "owed for `Keeps`" is owed by no rung: equal traces of two writings, of the converse and of the mirror image, halting where another machine halts, the empty side, and staying within a polynomial of R005. Their proofs for this engine (`EdgeSame`, `Settle`, `Transfer`, `Final`, `EmptySide`, `FromTabled`, `SameHalt`, `Mirror`, `EdgePure`, `EdgeMirror`, `Bounded`, `Keeps` under `Rungs/Symmetric/`), the tabled and mirrored machines and R005 are off master and in its history up to commit `3c03bb3`. Halting on more inputs, and halting when one side of a join is empty, are candidate improvements for later rungs. Not proved: any bound on what the engine costs in the size of the input.

**Revision, 2026-11-16 (seventeenth): what the engine holds.** Space is now the cells of the state a machine holds between two steps and what the step in progress allocates (`ADMISSION.md`, second revision of this date), and an engine has to list what its state holds and prove the state is determined by the listing. The engine's listing is `heldOf` (`Rungs/Symmetric/Machine.lean`): every table with its goal, its answers, its strategies with their cursors and its counters, the edges read, and the counters of the state. Nothing was changed in what the engine does. It never removes a table or an answer, and a cursor keeps every pair it has combined (`Cursor.done`), so what it holds only grows; these are what a rung admitted by a win in space can let go of. Point 5 of the list of assumptions below describes the count before this date.

**Revision, 2026-11-16 (eighteenth): the reading of edges is a part the engine is given.** The machine takes the reading of an edge as an argument (`EdgeReader`, `engine u rd`), as it takes the unfolding race. The proofs ask of a reading one thing, `ReadOk`: when it returns interfaces every answer of the node ends or begins inside one of them, and from the depth `readNeed` on it returns something. Rung 1 is the engine with `runsReader`, the reading described above, and nothing about it has changed. The reason is a cost found by reading `runs`: it composes every product of the alternatives at an edge before anything can remove one, so a side `P_1 ; ... ; P_k ; F ; c` with each `P_i` a union of two spans costs `2 ^ k` compositions at the first round of its goal, whatever follows. A reading that carries only the interfaces can replace it without touching the rest.

**Revision, 2026-11-16 (nineteenth): a reading from the far end, run beside the first.** `faces` (`Rungs/Symmetric/Machine.lean`) reads the same edge from the other end of the node and carries interfaces only: given the interfaces of what follows a node, the interfaces of the node followed by it. A span is composed with each interface and its own interface is read; a union joins what its members show; a chain passes the interfaces from its far member to its near one; a call is followed once, and a call being read, like a meet, shows the interface that restricts nothing. Two products with the same interface are one interface. Read off the definitions and not yet proved: on `P_1 ; ... ; P_k ; F ; c`, with `F` a ground span, it makes a number of compositions linear in `k` where `runs` makes `2 ^ k`; and neither way is the cheaper on every node, since on `F ; S_1 ; ... ; S_k ; c`, with each `S_i` the converse of a `P_i`, the far end shows `2 ^ k` interfaces of which `F` lets one through, while `runs` makes a number linear in `k`. So `readBoth` runs the two side by side with the kernel's `race`, which costs at most seven times the cheaper, and returns what the first to finish returns. What a reading returns can therefore depend on what the kernel charges; the proofs never asked that it should not. Proved, with nothing run (`Rungs/Symmetric/Faces.lean`): what `faces` returns is right about the node followed by what it was given (`faces_ok`), it is boundaries (`faces_bound`), and from the depth `readNeed` on it returns something (`faces_some`); so `bothReader` is a reading the machine can be given (`good_bothReader`), and the engine with it, `bothWays`, has the laws and the bound on direction (`Rung.lean`). It is not a rung of the ladder: nothing is yet proved about what it costs.

**Revision, 2026-11-17 (twentieth): the two readings are run within a budget.** `readBoth` is gone. Read off the definitions and not proved: a node can be dear to read from both ends (a chain of unions of spans that move a constructor from one side of a pair to the other shows as many interfaces at either end as it has products), the machine reads the sides of a split in one piece while it prepares a goal, and a union gives each round to one member; so beside such a node, in a union, a goal waited for the whole reading, however cheap the goal was. `readCapped` runs the two readings with the kernel's `within`, on `readCap depth` ticks, one more each time the depth doubles, and gives up as it does when the depth is not enough: the machine then goes on without the check and reads again at twice the depth, as before. The depth doubles on every reading that gives up and one goal makes several, so a budget that doubled with it would not bound one round; this one grows by a tick. A reading may now give up because of what it has spent, so the depth from which it does not is the reading's own (`ReadOk` takes it; `GoodReader` asks that there be one). Proved, with nothing run (`Rungs/Symmetric/Capped.lean`): a depth that is enough for `runs` is as good as any greater, value and cost (`runs_stable`), so the cost of the reading stops growing while the budget does not, and `cappedReader` is a reading the machine can be given (`good_cappedReader`); the engine with it, `bothWays`, has the laws and the bound on direction. Nothing is yet proved about what it costs.

### What a step is

A step is one request to the root and emits the answers the root table gained, usually none. Two writings of one input give the same sequence of steps, so the trace properties hold with equality and not only up to `TraceEquiv`.

## 4. The candidates compared

Against M6's exit criteria. "Exact" means the property holds by construction; "no" means it fails on a stated input.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| `SeqAssoc` | No: the tables split at the written node | Exact for `;` (the pipe is flat) | Exact | Exact |

**Keeping a hedge.** Rejected. It cannot give L29: there are exponentially many bracketings, and a hedge against a machine that reads the written bracketing does not have `SeqAssoc`. It is also not needed for symmetry once F1 is used.

**Two-ended alternation.** The flat pipe with a boundary at each end is the right data structure and is point 1 of the decision for `;`. As an evaluation order it is rejected. It is driving from the ends, which `r005/design-notes.md` finding 1 and the rung 6 thread's `digits d` family (over 100 times R004) show loses to joining tables on some inputs, and section 5.2 shows it is exponential when the restriction is in the middle. This is the recorded outcome for row R6 of the old engine's optimization inventory (ledger L25): the flattening is carried into the verified engine, the alternation is superseded by the balance.

**Full nets.** Rejected for M6, for three reasons. No end-state item needs more than associativity and commutativity, which the tree normal form gives. A sub-net of a net can have more than two interfaces, so tables would stop being binary relations. And the table key would be a net up to isomorphism, which makes canonical labelling of hypergraphs a kernel operation that must be costed on the base machine; the tree normal form is canonical by sorting. What nets would add (full reducers S15, many-sided meets S39, residual calls in answers for S22) is uncommitted, and the generic answer type of L28 keeps the last of these open.

## 7. Open points and risks

**O1. The simulation theorem (F3).** *Attacked twice on 2026-11-13. False for the engine as first designed. False for the engine of the third revision too: the ladder family is polynomial for R005 and exponential for that engine under every request rule it had (simulation note 9.2). Open for the engine of the fourth revision, which is polynomial on every family measured. For it O1 reduces to: the written line is charged additively (G1, argued), what a group really spends is polynomial in its level (G2, open: in the worst case the factor is the number of splits per level of nesting, and no family was found that realises it), and the interface check finishes what R005's unfolding race finishes (Lemma U, open). The text below is the statement as it first stood.* With shared tables, a strategy can be delayed by answers that another strategy of a sub-goal produced earlier than R005 would have. The direct proof bounds that delay by a polynomial per level of the derivation, which compounds with depth and is not a polynomial. Whether a fixed polynomial holds is not known. Three facts make it plausible: a complete table stops its other strategies; R005's cost is dominated by its unfolding race, which reads no bracketing, so "the cheapest writing" varies by a constant on the families measured; and the prototype is within a polynomial of the best fixed bracketing on every family tried. If it fails, the hedge is not a fallback (section 4), and the conflict between `SeqAssoc` and `Simulates` goes to Anthony under roadmap rule 3 with the counterexample. M6 should attack O1 first, on paper and on a prototype with recursion, before any Lean.

**O2. Cost of exhaustion.** *Measured 2026-11-13: on family C without pushes at `k = 10` the share rule halts at 348149 ticks (exponent about 4.8) and the flat round at 56043 (about 3.3). With pushes the flat round is exponential and the share rule is not (simulation note 3.3).* The prototype halts at about the fifth power of the chain length on family C. Completion is reached only through a strategy whose sub-tables are complete, and every interval's share is divided again at each level. A direct share for every opened goal is the first thing to try.

**O3. Meets.** The strategies for `&` are pushes only. A join of two groups of members for every grouping is exponential in the number of members. Whether pushes alone simulate R005's join of two written sides is not checked.

**O4. Push strategies open one goal per answer.** The balance among a goal's strategies is then over a growing set. R001 has the same growth; its effect on the balance is not measured.

**O5. The orientation in point 2 is arbitrary.** It makes mirror cost exact and adds nothing to cost robustness, which comes only from the balance. If the balance were dropped, the engine would still pass the mirror criterion and would be bad on half its inputs. The admission requirement that catches this is `Simulates` against R005, not `DirectionFree`.

**O6. The request rule.** *Settled by measurement on 2026-11-13 (fourth revision), not by proof.* The split balance is polynomial on the middle-restricted family with pushes, on fair OR with several members that never finish, on counting down and on the ladder. The two rules that had been left untried were tried and fail (simulation note 9.3). What stays open about the rule: G2 above; the fairness bound in ticks on a table that grows exponentially with no boundary (the strings family: `2k + 4` requests, but ticks grow six times per two steps, as for whole rounds; no rule measured has the tick form on both that family and fair OR with three endless members); its cost on the middle-restricted family (the sixth power, where the share rule had the fourth); and what the bookkeeping of levels costs, which the prototype does not charge. *Corrected 2026-11-15: an earlier note here said Anthony had decided that the fairness bound is stated in requests. He says he never decided it. Ticks or requests is open, and so is the strings family as a point about the rule.*

**O7. The interface check is not proved to cover the unfolding race** (Lemma U of the simulation note). *Settled on 2026-11-15: it does not (simulation note, section 11). The engine hosts the race itself; see the fourth revision of 2026-11-15 in section 3. The text below is the point as it stood.* Its recursion guard unfolds each call once. Since the fourth revision it is offered only for a side that contains a call; that this loses nothing is argued, not proved. On 200 random small programs the engine halts wherever R005 halts within its budget (138 of them) and on 35 more; none of those programs needs the check, so this says more about `TerminatesWhere` than about the check (`scripts/symmetric_engine_halting.py`).

**Revision, 2026-11-13: what the restated kernel provides.** The `Kernel`, L30 and L31 rows are delivered in `Trusted/Kernel.lean`, `Trusted/CostMonad.lean`, `Kernel/NormalForm.lean` and `Kernel/Derivation.lean`. Five points differ from, or add to, what the table says.

1. Two classes. `Kernel S M` keeps the operations on spans, with `metered`, and without `canon`, `eqRel`, `size` and `idSpan`. `DerivationKernel S N A M` extends it with nodes and answers. An engine is written against the second.
2. `size` and `idSpan` leave the kernel (L44). On a hash-consed store the size of a term as a tree is not a word the machine holds, and storing it at allocation would need unbounded arithmetic per node. The balance reads costs with `metered`. An absent boundary is the identity and a chain has at least two members, so no identity span is needed.
3. Nodes of the normal form are expressions in a fixed shape (`RelOf.norm`): chains flattened, meets sorted as multisets, unions sorted as sets, converse only on calls. Adjacent spans of a chain are not fused. The converse of a node is `normConv`, and a call node carries a flag for the converse of its body. `sem_norm` is the theorem that the normal form denotes what the expression denotes.
4. The rules are `leaf`, `simp`, `join`, `meetA`, `inj`, `unfold`, `swapA`. `join` takes an answer of a stretch with its right boundary absent and an answer of the next stretch whose left boundary is absent or the right interface of the first (a push), or the mirror image of that. `meetA` takes the second answer between the same boundaries or between the interfaces of the first. `simp` is a rule because simplification by the theory is how a dead answer is recognised; it does not change what the answer denotes among the pairs the theory allows.
5. Space is `peak` and `held` in `Cost`. Only `within` releases cells (the abandoned side of a race), so an engine that never abandons has `peak = held` growing with its tables, as the L31 row expects. There is no depth component: stacks are explicit and live in the store (L43), so depth is space.

Provided since (roadmap L47, `Kernel/NormalFormStable.lean`): the normal form is stable (`norm` of a node is the node, and the converse of the converse of a node is the node), which is what lets a goal and its converse share one table. Not provided, and needed by this design before M6: charges for the operations on nodes that are derived on the store instead of postulated (roadmap L45).
